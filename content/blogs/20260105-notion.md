I want to preface that Notion is a good software, but the free version kinda sucks...

## What happened?

We have **hit the limit of free Notion blocks**, yippee... *(sarcastically, negatively)*

![[free-blocks.png]]

For context, the [old version](https://hddmtm-dnd.notion.site/1be318388a888019b571e438911b4927?v=221318388a88805a9b5f000cdee7507c) of the Eden wiki is created using Notion. It's easy to use, intuitive, and (quite) customizable, especially the text colors[^1]. I have used Obsidian to keep my notes before too, but the main downside is that Obsidian Sync is a paid subscription. Since Notion notes can be synced across multiple devices (my laptop, phone, and tablet), has the ability to easily publish to a website, and can be shared with my friends (shoutout to the *Flamingon* crews) for editing using Notion Workspace, it was a no-brainer which to pick. *Until it became a problem.*

It has not occurred to me that the free version has a block limit which, y'know, is a very important thing for a growing wiki. What do you mean there's a block limit!? ***WHY*** is that a thing!? Is it to bait me to get comfy then bleed my wallets dry? For f\*ck sake!

Look, for what it's worth, Notion has been the easiest note-taking app I've used so far at that point. It allows me to drag and drop each block and organize them to my heart's content. I enjoy formatting my page's layouts, it's fun! Also, it has more capabilities than raw Markdown files, which is a huge plus, like coloring.[^2] (Coloring in `.md` and `.html` files is hard.[^3] Or maybe I'm just dumb, idk.) It's like if [Wix](https://www.wix.com/) and `README.md` had a child. But of course, I shouldn't expect those high-capability software to come for free. Heck, in this day and age, finding something that doesn't have subscription-based models is like finding an atom that used to belong to Einstein's nose hair.

So, what's the solution?

## Solution 1: Panic

Because I have no idea how I would share my notes without bleeding my pockets.[^4] I'm currently a university student, which means I'm broke. I'm also a southeast Asian, which means I'm stingy as f\*ck. And most of all, I'm *very* picky when it comes to passion projects like this. I want to make it good, and by gods I *will* find the easiest and cheapest (preferably free) way.

Here's the list of what I wanted:

- I want to keep the wiki style for Eden. It's cool, and my internship has taught me that the best base is not the [second base](https://redkiwiapp.com/en/english-guide/questions/1qa1v0UIQmHva30qeVZL). It's knowledge base.
	- Wiki is also the best way to store and traverse these kind of things.
- I want to share this with other people in the future, so hosting the wiki on the internet would be the easiest way.
- I want to easily move my Notion notes to the new home. Anything that uses Markdown would be the best.
- I do **not** want to touch JavaScript.[^5]
- I don't really want to change the workflow and file structures I already have. *(This one we'll talk about later in the blog.)*

As you can see, my pickiness is enough for anyone who have worked as customer consultant before have a migraine.[^6]

## The Great Wiki Hunt of 2025

I started looking for an alternative to Notion since September 2025. Not because I wanted to ditch it, but because I didn't know about the Wiki feature yet. And then when I discovered the feature, I stopped the search immediately because I'm a lazy b\*tch.

I remembered consulting a list, but not sure which one. It's probably [the list on MediaWiki](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Hosting_services).

At that time, all the alternatives I've found has some restrictions that were deal-breakers for me. For example:

- [Miraheze](https://miraheze.org/) is a wiki farm that is free with no ads, which is nice. But for obvious reasons, they wouldn't just let you request to host any wikis. Eden is a personal project, so it probably wouldn't pass.
- [wiki.gg](https://www.wiki.gg/) is primarily for video games. Eden won't pass the application if I'm a reviewer.
- [Wikidot](https://www.wikidot.com/) is very promising. [The DND 5e community wiki](https://dnd5e.wikidot.com/) is also hosted on there. (Very cool site.) However, I have a gut feeling that it would be a little difficult to use and customize.
- I've considered [WordPress](https://wordpress.com/) once, but ultimately didn't go with it. I don't remember why.
- Also considered using Google Docs, but formatting is a pain. And if you've been reading, you know I love formatting stuff into eye candy.
- Fandom is so sh\*t I won't even link it.

I haven't heard of Quartz back then. Probably because I was mainly looking for "wiki hosting" services. In hindsight, that was a mistake.

## The Urge for Alternatives and the Lucky Coincidence

Fast forward to late December of 2025, right after Christmas. I was looking for a map making program. (I'll probably will write about this in another blog post.)[^7] As any person would do when they have no idea what the f\*ck they're doing, I turn to researching on YouTube.

Long story short, I stumble upon a video from [Power Word Spill](https://www.youtube.com/@powerwordspill) about [comparing map making programs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nwCWfvzuUSA), and then about his video about [free D&D campaign management app](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DBgWB1NF7hY). Spoiler alert, it's Obsidian.

I sigh, but watched the video anyways. Obsidian Sync, yeah it's paid subscription, I can't afford that monthly... Wait, what did he just mentioned? **Quartz**?

## Striking Gold (or in this case, Quartz)

At the [7:44 minute mark of that video](https://youtu.be/DBgWB1NF7hY?t=464), he mentioned [**Quartz**](https://quartz.jzhao.xyz/). Oh my god, how haven't I heard of this?

In the video, he did mention that the setup is quite daunting. But if you know anything about computer science students, daunting is a word used as an invitation. Sifting through documentations is a normal day.[^8] After about a day, the first version of this very wiki is up.

Moving notes from Notion to Obsidian, however, is a unique kind of pain. I've gotten used to the workflow of Notion so much that migrating at first feels very ugly. However, as Sun Tzu had said in the Art of War, *"those who are afraid of changes are weak \*ss b\*tches"* or something idk.[^9]

I ended up breaking up my notes and organize them in a much better way, in my humble opinion. Using Quartz has been really cool, and it's satisfying to customize as long as you know some TypeScript.[^10] Their [Discord](https://discord.gg/cRFFHYye7t) also has many cool community plugins which may interest anyone who are reading this and want to share their own [digital garden](https://maggieappleton.com/garden-history).

It also made me learn something about web developments too... Oh, **F\*CK**![^11]

## What's Next?

I'm officially announcing that we are finally **ditching the Notion version of this wiki**. It will no longer be updated or maintained. I will slowly move my resources to my Obsidian vaults, and will publish stuffs using Quartz and GitHub. I'm glad to say, I've never been happier to take notes.

This blog is not an advertisement to any of the products mentioned in this post. I've not been paid. I'm still broke. But my recommendations are genuine, and I do think that you should give them a try.

That's all. I hope to see you again.[^12]
